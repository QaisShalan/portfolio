Conclusions & analysis

The results strongly confirm that e-commerce is now mainstream: 85% of respondents have made at least one online purchase, and the majority buy at least once a month — indicating deeply habitual behavior rather than occasional novelty. This mirrors global trends; according to Statista, over 2.7 billion people shopped online worldwide in 2025, a number forecast to keep climbing. Statista 2025

Younger respondents (18–39) make up 75% of our sample and are the most frequent buyers — purchasing weekly or monthly. This aligns with Pew Internet research showing adults under 50 shop online at substantially higher rates than older groups and are far more likely to use mobile devices to browse and purchase. Pew Internet

Education correlates positively with online purchasing: 70% of respondents hold a college degree or higher, and nearly all of those had purchased online. This tracks with eMarketer data suggesting higher-educated consumers are more confident with digital payments and product research. eMarketer

Amazon's dominance at 50% is unsurprising given its global logistics and trust infrastructure. Notably, Trendyol ranked second at 20% — reflecting the local Turkish market context of this survey, where Trendyol is the leading domestic platform. This underscores how regional platforms can rival global giants in local markets, a trend ClickZ has covered extensively. ClickZ

The 10% who browsed but never purchased represent a persistent trust or friction gap. Barriers frequently cited in E-Commerce Times research include payment security concerns, difficulty returning items, and a preference to see products in person before buying. Reducing these friction points remains a central challenge for online retailers. E-Commerce Times
